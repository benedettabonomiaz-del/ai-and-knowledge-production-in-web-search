# AI and Knowledge Production in Web Search
### The Epistemic Shift from Information Retrieval to Knowledge Generation

Author: **Benedetta Bonomi**  
Academic Context: **Sociology of AI (A.Y. 2025/26)** — *M.Sc. in Human-Centered AI*

## Executive Summary

For decades, the Internet functioned as a decentralized **Information Retrieval (IR)** system where users actively synthesized knowledge from a web of links. The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into search architectures (e.g., Google SGE, Perplexity, ChatGPT) marks a fundamental epistemic shift toward **Knowledge Generation (KG)**. 

Search engines are transitioning from **portals of links to answer machines**. The user shifts from an active synthesizer to a passive receiver of statistically probable summaries.

Drawing on **Allan Dafoe's AI Governance Framework**, this research evaluates how AI search infrastructures reshape knowledge availability, user agency, and public discourse when confronted with controversial socio-political topics.

---

## Theoretical Framework

* **AI as Socio-Technical Infrastructure (Dafoe, 2018/2025):** LLM search engines are not neutral utility tools; they are transformative infrastructures whose design, data constraints, and corporate alignment shape societal epistemic agency.
* **Infrastructural Bias:** LLM bias is not merely a technical bug in ranking, but an inherent product of data selection, Human Feedback (RLHF), and corporate risk aversion that tends to flatten political dissent into moderate, synthetic consensus.
* **The Sustainability Crisis & "Free-Riding":** LLMs intercept web traffic by synthesizing content directly in the chat interface. This dismantles the traditional *Traffic Incentive*, starving primary creators/journalists of visits and threatening the open web with model collapse.
* **Hard vs. Soft Determinism:** Traditional Search (SERP) allowed for *soft determinism*, leaving room for user navigation and critical negotiation. Generative AI accelerates toward *hard determinism*, where algorithmically generated answers centralize authority and discourage verification.

---

## Methodology & Metrics

The empirical study tests the outputs of **Google SERP**, **ChatGPT-5.2**, and **Perplexity Pro (Best Mode)** across **8 sensitive socio-political and economic queries** (e.g., colonial reparations, death penalty, gender quotas, border policy, online censorship).

Each response was evaluated on a **0 to 5 scale** across six core metrics:

1. **Implicit Ideological Orientation:** Favouring specific political framings through word choice/emphasis.
2. **Normative Tone:** Moral judgments or ethical assertions vs. neutral factual description.
3. **Risk Aversion:** Use of disclaimers, hedging, or refusal to avoid controversial positions.
4. **Pluralism:** Visibility and balance of competing perspectives vs. single dominant framing.
5. **Source Transparency:** Verifiability and clarity of information origins through explicit citations.
6. **Traffic Incentive:** Extent to which response design encourages clicking external sources.

---

## Key Findings & Comparative Results

### 1. Google SERP (Traditional Information Retrieval)
* **High Pluralism (5/5) & Source Transparency (5/5):** Preserves user epistemic agency by acting as a passive index of existing web conflicts.
* **Zero Normative Bias (0/5):** Does not synthesize or deliver a single moral verdict.

### 2. ChatGPT-5.2 (Conversational Synthesis)
* **High Risk Aversion (5/5) & Normative Tone (4.1/5):** Acts as a diplomatic mediator, flattening radical perspectives into moderate, polite bullet points.
* **Zero Source Transparency (0/5) & Traffic Incentive (0/5):** Fully retains knowledge within the chat interface, severing referral traffic to original content.

### 3. Perplexity AI (Citation-Backed Answer Engine)
* **High Source Transparency (5/5) with Low Traffic Incentive (1.8/5):** Citations serve as "expert witnesses" to validate the AI's answer rather than gateways to external web exploration ("Killing the Link").
* **Strong Normative Assertions (4.5/5):** Frequently takes explicit institutional stances, acting as an authoritative judge rather than a neutral portal.
